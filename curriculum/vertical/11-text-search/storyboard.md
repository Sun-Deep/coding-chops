# Storyboard

One sweep, then one page.

## The shot map

```text
0-300     find bar, headings, and six panels sweeping the same page together
308-322   the grid gives way to one page
322-402   Boyer-Moore is walked across it, window jumping over unread ground
402-415   the same page floods with what a straight scan touched
415-419   hold
```

## Frame zero has to answer three questions

The first cut answered none of them and the creator caught all three: what word
is being searched for, what these six names are, and what the video is showing.

The phrase was nowhere in the frame at all. The panels highlighted the hits and
nothing said what the hits were, which is the kind of omission that is invisible
to whoever built it and immediate to everybody else.

So frame zero now carries a find bar: a rounded field, a magnifier, the phrase
and a result count. It is drawn as the thing a viewer already owns rather than
as a label, because recognising a find-in-page bar answers what this is and what
it is hunting in one glance. It stays up for the whole cut.

Above the grid, two headings: READ EVERY CHARACTER over the left column and
SKIP AHEAD over the right. The columns were always the argument and the pictures
always made it, but not until halfway through, and the narration did not name it
until three seconds in.

And the note beside each panel name is now what the matcher does in plain words
rather than a shorter version of its surname. "Boyer-Moore" is a reference
somebody can look up later. "Jumps on two rules" is what they need in the second
they have. Searching is household; Horspool is not, and no amount of animation
fixes a name nobody knows.

The grid gave up 52 pixels for all of this and still clears the narration.

## The clock is position, not work

Boyer-Moore is finished after eight per cent of the total reads. A clock
counting work would stop three of the six panels inside the first two seconds
and hold them for nine, which is the failure VR10 had to design around.

On position, all six sweep the page together and finish together, and what
separates them is how much of it they had to light up. The cut is a coverage
picture rather than a race, and that is the better fit: the claim is not that
Boyer-Moore is quick, it is that it does not need to look.

## The hero is the grid, not a panel

Left column reads every character, right column does not. By the end the two
columns are two different pictures, and the claim is made before a number has
been read. That is the whole reason there are six rather than two: one against
one is an anecdote, three against three is a pattern.

The three in the left column are near identical to each other and that is not a
fault to be designed away. It is the finding, and the narration says it.

## The highlight is the one a reader already owns

A character a matcher looked at is lit and one it never looked at is not. That
is what Ctrl+F does to a page, so the panel needs no explaining.

Banded by when rather than drawn in one colour, so the sweep has a warm leading
edge and the panels are not six identical wipes. The first cut of this ran the
ramp hot across most of its range and the left column came out as a solid block
of accent rather than a highlight; the hot band is narrow now and the accent
reads as a leading edge.

## The verdict

The grid says what happened and one page says why, because the mechanism cannot
be seen at panel size.

The window is drawn as a box around the eleven characters the pattern is lined
up against, and on a mismatch it does not slide one place, it leaps. The ground
it crosses is never read and stays dark behind it.

Its read characters wear the accent here rather than the cold neutral the panels
use, because on one page the claim has to be carried by how little of the page
is marked at all, and the first cut of this drew them in a dim grey that carried
nothing.

Then the last beat: the same page floods with what a straight scan touched. The
readout says 2,390 against 371 and without the flood it shows only the 371,
which is a number with nothing to look at. The flood finishes the claim with a
picture.

## Cue map

Underneath, the reading: a `probe` every thirty-fourth character a panel looks
at, pitched by the column the sweep is in so each line of the page is a run up
a pentatonic scale. The panels fire out of phase with each other, because they
are all on one sweep and a stride counted from zero would have six strike
together and then go quiet, which is the bug VR10 shipped and had to fix.

Density carries the claim. Naive looks at nine characters a frame and
Boyer-Moore at one, so the left column is a stream and the right column a
scatter, and which side is doing less is audible before either number is read.

Over the top:

```text
308   appear   the grid gives way
402   land     the walk reaches the end of the page
402   fill     the straight scan's coverage floods in
```

And through the walk, the top of the scale every time the window leaps rather
than slides, which is the one event in the cut worth hearing.

## Colour

Six lanes is past the four the standard allows to carry their own hue, so this
is a one-accent cut. The ramp on read ground runs from a neutral slate to the
accent, which is one hue and not two.

The closing flood is drawn in that same neutral slate rather than a second
colour. A straight scan reading everything is not a second kind of thing, it is
the absence of the skipping the accent marks.
