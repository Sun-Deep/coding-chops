# Vertical 21: which elevator gets you there first

Status: built, not posted

## What it claims

The same seven people call one elevator at the same seconds, in two buildings.
The left car serves calls in the order the buttons were pressed, one rider at a
time. The right car sweeps: it keeps going the way it is going, stops for
anyone getting out and anyone waiting to go its way, and turns round only when
nothing is left ahead.

You are the fifth caller, on floor 6, going down to 3. The sweep has you out
in 1:01. In order takes 1:53. By the time the in-order car lets you out, the
sweep has already delivered all seven.

The catch: three of the seven got out later with the sweep than they would
have in order, one of them by 48 seconds. The sweep is better on average, not
for everyone.

## Why this topic

Picked from the Instagram Explore study of 2026-09-30. The reels that went
furthest were everyday subjects with almost nothing to read, and a side by side
with one thing different. Everybody has waited for a lift and watched it go
the wrong way. The subject needs no background, and the question in the
headline is one a viewer can guess at before anything moves.

## Scope

It teaches that serving requests in arrival order, which feels fair, makes
nearly everybody wait longer than serving them in the order the building lays
them out, and why: the in-order car carries one person at a time and crosses
the building for each, while the sweep carries everybody going its way at once.

It does not model how real controllers assign several cars, capacity limits,
acceleration, or destination dispatch. One car, unlimited capacity, a fixed
time per floor and per stop. The in-order car is a thought experiment, not a
description of any product.

One scenario is one sample. Over a thousand others the sweep had the shorter
mean trip in 998 and travelled fewer floors in all 1,000; the median ratio of
mean trips was 1.52. Seed 1 sits at 1.41, below the median.
