# Vertical 24: what's the fastest way to board a plane

Status: built, not posted

## What it claims

Three identical cabins, 12 rows of six, the same 72 passengers with the same
bags, called in three orders. Back to front, in three zones, takes 6:37.
Random takes 5:16. Window seats first, then middles, then aisles, takes 4:17.

The cause is where people stop. Back to front sends everybody in a zone to the
same four rows, so they queue behind each other while one at a time stows a
bag. Window first spreads them over the whole cabin, so a dozen people can be
stowing at once.

## Why this topic

Everyone has stood in the aisle behind somebody's bag, and most people assume
back to front is the sensible order because it sounds tidy. That gives the
reel something to disagree with. The shape is the elevator and checkout one:
the same people under different rules, side by side, finishing on a clock.

## Scope

A one-second model: walk a row a second, no passing, 4 to 12 seconds to stow
a bag for three in four passengers, 5 seconds for every seated passenger who
has to get up. No families boarding together, no priority groups, no
late arrivals, no bins filling up. The cabin is 12 rows, the size of the mock
757 Steffen and Hotchkiss boarded with 72 real passengers, where block
boarding from the back was the slowest of five methods.

Over 1,000 seeds window first was fastest in 999 and random beat back to front
in 916. The ranking held at 30 rows, with passengers taking two rows of aisle,
and with five zones.
