# Vertical 25: should you circle for a closer parking spot

Status: posted

## What it claims

Two drivers reach the same busy car park at the same moment. One picks the
row in front of the door and takes the open stall nearest the store. The
other will only park in the six rows by the store and loops the aisles until
one is open as it passes. Meanwhile cars leave at random and other drivers
arriving take the free stall nearest the door, so the close stalls that open
are usually gone again before the circler comes back round.

On the trip shown the row picker is through the door in 0:56. The circler
parks one row from the store after driving 740 m and is through the door at
4:31. Over 400 trips at the same settings, circling lost 310.

## The trip shown, and why

Not the first seed. A typical circling trip is only a couple of seconds slower
than picking a row (medians 57 s and 55 s); what circling costs is the long
tail, where one circler in ten is still at it after 4:36. The reel shows that
tail on purpose: `RANK=1` picks the seed whose row time is nearest the row
median and whose circle time is nearest the circle 90th percentile. The
payoff line states the frequency over 400 trips, not this trip.

## Why this topic

Everyone who drives has circled a car park, and most people argue for one
side. It is a US and European everyday, the audience the plane reel found.
It matches Velkey's field study at a Walmart, where drivers who cycled for a
better spot spent more total time getting into the store than those who took
the closest open space in a row.

## Scope

A one-second model: 3 m/s driving, 1.4 m/s walking, 6 s to pull in, cars
staying 30 minutes on average, another car arriving every 15 s and taking the
best free stall. Circling lost in 259 to 368 of 400 trips across eight other
settings. Against taking the very first open stall you pass, circling can win,
so the reel does not make that comparison.
