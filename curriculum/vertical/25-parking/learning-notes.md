# Learning notes

## Why circling loses

A close stall only helps if you are the one passing it when it opens. The
circler is one car among everyone arriving, and arriving drivers head straight
for the best free stall, so most close stalls that open go to them. Each
missed chance costs the circler a full loop of the car park. On the trip shown
it drove 740 m to save a walk of a few rows.

## Why the typical trip is close and the average is not

Most of the time a close stall opens within the first loop and circling loses
by a few seconds, or wins. Some of the time several loops pass with nothing,
and that trip costs minutes. The median hides that; the 90th percentile does
not (276 s circling against 86 s picking a row).

## Where it flips

If the circler accepts ten rows instead of six, or the near rows start
emptier, circling wins more often, though it still lost 259 and 264 of 400.
If it only accepts three rows, it lost 368 of 400.

## What the model leaves out

Pedestrians, other circlers blocking the aisle, disabled bays, cars waiting
for a spot with the indicator on, and drivers who give up and take a far spot.
