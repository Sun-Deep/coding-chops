# Vertical 26: should everyone stand on the escalator

Status: built, not posted

## What it claims

On a tall escalator in the rush hour, standing on both sides moves more
people than standing on the right and walking on the left. Transport for
London tested it at Holborn for six months: about 115 people a minute with a
walking lane, about 141 to 151 with both sides standing, roughly 30% more.

The reel shows why. Walkers climb single file and need room to climb, and
they slow on a long climb, so the walking lane carries fewer people than a
lane of people standing a step and a half apart. On the left escalator the
walking lane runs with gaps; on the right both lanes are packed, and the
count at the top pulls ahead.

## Why this topic

Every metro, mall and airport has escalators, and "stand right, walk left"
is etiquette people defend. The result says the walkers are slowing everyone
down, which is the argument the plane reel drew comments from, and here it
comes with a real trial behind it, not only a model.

## Scope

Tall escalators only, and the busiest part of the rush hour. TfL found
standing only helps above about an 18.5 m rise; on short escalators more
people want to walk and a walking lane carries more. The model does not
reproduce that reversal robustly, so the reel does not claim it; the caption
says the result is for tall escalators.

The simulation is calibrated to Holborn, not independent of it: its two gaps
are chosen so it carries what TfL measured. What it adds is the mechanism, on
screen, and that the result holds over 50 seeds and over a range of walker
spacing (11% to 39% more).
