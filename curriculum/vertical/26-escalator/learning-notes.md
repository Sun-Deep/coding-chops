# Learning notes

## Why standing moves more people

An escalator's capacity is people per metre of step times the speed of the
step. Standers pack in at about one per 0.62 m, a step and a half. Walkers
move faster, but each needs about 1.4 m clear to climb, and on a 23 m rise
they tire and slow; nobody can pass, so the lane moves at its slowest
climber's pace. Faster per person, fewer people.

## Why only on tall escalators

On a short escalator walkers do not tire, keep up their pace and close up,
and more people want to walk. TfL found the crossover at about an 18.5 m
rise: in 2002, 44% walked on the tallest escalators and 53 to 61% on the
shortest.

## Why it was hard to make stick

At Holborn people kept walking in week one; standing only was enforced with
staff and signs. The capacity gain is real; persuading people is the
problem.

## What the model leaves out

People who refuse to stand, bags, people joining from the side, and the queue
at the bottom being fed unevenly between lanes.
