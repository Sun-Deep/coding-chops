# Sources

- Gridlock, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gridlock
  Checked 2026-10-05: the term was made popular by New York traffic
  engineer Sam Schwartz in his 1980 Gridlock Prevention Plan; the classic
  cause is drivers entering a junction on green without room on the other
  side, and being trapped there when the light changes.
- Daganzo, C. F. (2007). Urban gridlock: macroscopic modeling and mitigation
  approaches. Transportation Research Part B 41(1), 49-62.
  https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:transb:v:41:y:2007:i:1:p:49-62
  Models how a neighbourhood's traffic collapses into gridlock once it holds
  too many cars.
- Operational evaluation of Do Not Block the Box campaigns, TRID:
  https://trid.trb.org/view/1573335 and Analysis of vehicle blocking
  behavior on intersection performance: https://trid.trb.org/view/1439541
  Before and after study at six Atlanta junctions: markings did not
  significantly change behaviour, and more than half of drivers still blocked
  when they had the chance; blocking can spread to nearby junctions and, in
  extreme cases, lead to gridlock.
- Coffman, E. G., Elphick, M. J. and Shoshani, A. (1971). System deadlocks.
  ACM Computing Surveys 3(2), 67-78. The four conditions for a deadlock.
- Treiber, M., Hennecke, A. and Helbing, D. (2000). Congested traffic states
  in empirical observations and microscopic simulations. Physical Review E
  62, 1805. The Intelligent Driver Model the cars follow.
- The run: `scripts/measure-gridlock.mjs`, which imports
  `src/vertical/27-gridlock/simulation.ts`, the same module the reel draws.
