# Vertical 30: why do you both step the same way

Status: built, not posted

## What it claims

When two people meet head on and move at the same moment, neither sees the
other's choice, so they can step to the same side, then both step back, and
mirror each other. Waiting a random moment after a bump breaks the tie: one
moves first, the other sees it, and they pass. Wi-Fi and Ethernet do exactly
this after two devices talk at once, and the name is random backoff.

In the model, of the pairs who would dance three or more times stepping at
once, the same people waiting a random beat after a bump average 1.35 dodges
instead of 4.65, and 93% get past in under 3. Over all pairs, long dances
fall from 12.4% to 1.9%.

## Why this topic

Everyone has done the hallway dance, and which side to keep to is an
argument people already have, which the lights reel lacked. Wi-Fi is a
computer everyone owns.

## Scope and honesty

The model is not calibrated to measured pedestrians: reaction times and the
window in which two moves count as simultaneous are assumptions, swept in
the script. The drop in long dances holds in every setting. Time to pass
improves in most and costs a few hundredths of a second in the two where
dances are already rare. If everyone keeps to the same side, nobody dances
at all, and that beats backoff.

The reel shows the same people in both corridors with the same reactions,
from the first pair in seed 1 whose first step is a bump. That pair dances
4 times stepping at once and 2 with the wait, milder than the average pair
who would dance.
