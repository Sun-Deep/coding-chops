# Vertical 32: do kiosks get you your food faster

Status: built, not posted

## What it claims

A burger counter is a pipeline: order, cook, collect. Replacing one cashier
with six self-order kiosks removes the wait to order, but every order still
goes through the same kitchen. So the line does not disappear; it moves to
the pickup counter. The time from the door to a tray falls by much less
than the ordering got faster. Speeding up one stage only helps until another
stage is the slowest, and that limit is Amdahl's law.

In the model, at the defaults, the wait to order falls from 4:24 to zero,
the wait for food after ordering rises from 1:31 to 4:10, and door to tray
falls from 6:50 to 5:24, 21% sooner. Three kiosks do as well as ten.

## Why this topic

Kiosks are something people already argue about: slower than a person,
faster, replacing jobs. The race runs until the payoff, the lesson of the
hallway reel: the till line on top and the crowd at the pickup counter
below keep changing until both trays come up. It is a new place, a
restaurant, after four road reels and a corridor.

## Scope and honesty

How long people take at a till, how much longer at a kiosk, and how fast
the kitchen works are assumptions; the script sweeps each. Kiosks are
faster in every setting, by 4% to 49%, and by most when the kitchen has
spare capacity and the till was the slow step. The ordering and cooking
times are not taken from any chain's data.

The reel follows customer #24 of seed 1, chosen as the one whose wait in
the till line is closest to the 20-rush average. It gets its tray in 6:48
at the till and 6:12 with kiosks, 9% sooner, less than the 21% average.
