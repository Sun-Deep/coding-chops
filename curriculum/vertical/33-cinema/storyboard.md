# Storyboard

One shot: the same cinema twice, one above the other, from the projection
booth. At frame 0 both rooms are half full, groups are walking in, and four
friends in orange stand in the right-hand aisle, one per step. By frame 106 everyone
before them has sat down; every free seat lights up, 23 in both rooms, and
the longest run is bracketed, 3 above and 10 below. At frame 136 the four
go: above, to four different seats, joined by a dashed orange line; below,
side by side in the back row. Then the rest arrive every 22 frames until
the house is full: above, two more groups are split up and get a chalk
dashed line, and the split count reaches 3; below, it stays at 0. The top
label becomes "Fragmentation" at frame 350.

## Built to read without the narration

The problem is shown before the result: the four stand still while the free
seats light up, so a viewer counts free seats and sees there is no run of
four before anyone moves. The split count above each room then shows the
same thing happening to other groups. The readout above each room says the same in
numbers. The split is drawn, not said: four orange people in four places,
joined by a line.

## How it got here

The first cut had the four friends walk in at about 8 seconds, after five
seconds of rooms filling, past the 0:04 point where recent reels lose the
most viewers. They are now on screen from frame 0 and sit by about 5
seconds, and the second half fills both rooms to a full house so the split
count keeps moving to the end. The first cut's room was plain; it now has
curved rows, high-backed seats with cup holders, velvet curtains, panelled
walls with lamps and exit signs, carpeted aisles and phone torches.

The first frames put the four friends in the bottom corner, clipped and
small. A walkway behind the back row then held them in the middle, but
drawn as heads and shoulders they read as a back row already seated. They
now stand in the right-hand aisle with bodies and legs, plainly waiting. The
projector beam first read as a hard grey wedge and is now blurred. The
payoff line wrapped to three lines and touched the lower room; it is now
two short lines. The reel first opened on two empty rooms; the first groups
are now already walking in at frame 0.

The creator asked for the real Avengers: Doomsday logo on the screen, for
the curiosity around its release. A studio logo in a monetised reel risks a
rights claim through Facebook's Rights Manager and implies a tie-in that
does not exist, so the screen plays an original film instead: NIGHTFALL, a
night city with searchlights, a caped figure on a roof, lightning that
lights the audience, and a title card. No real title, logo or lettering
style is used.

## Colour

Orange is one thing: you, the four friends. Seats are a deep cinema red,
clothes muted, the film night blue and steel.
