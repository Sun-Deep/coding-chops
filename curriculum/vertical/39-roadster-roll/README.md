# Vertical 39: how did the Tesla Roadster land upright

Status: posted 2026-10-18

## What it claims

At its reveal on 15 October 2026, Tesla's new Roadster drove onto the
underside of a curved ramp, held on by a fan sucking it to the surface,
let go, rolled over in the air on cold-gas thrusters and landed on its
wheels. The reel drops the same car twice from the same ramp, with the
car's instruments live in each panel. With the thrusters off it falls
without turning and lands on its roof 1.01 seconds later at 35.7 km/h, and
the roof crushing stops it at 26 g. With them on, one pair rolls it and the
opposite pair stops the roll, and that has to be done by 0.72 seconds,
because the jets underneath then need the rest of the fall to brake it at
2 g; it lands on its wheels at 1.33 seconds and never feels more than 2 g.

## Why this topic

The clip was everywhere the week it was posted (one reupload had 5.5
million views in two days), and the question under it is the obvious one:
how does a car let go upside down and land the right way up? The reel
answers with the fall clock, which anyone can check.

## Scope and honesty

The car is a solid box 4.4 by 2.0 by 1.15 m, the drop 5 m, the braking
thrust twice the car's weight, the touchdown 1 m/s, the roof crush 0.2 m
and the suspension travel 0.1 m. Tesla published
none of these, so the script sweeps each; the roll has to be done in under
a second at every setting, and the roof hits at 11 to 51 g against at most
4 g with the jets. The car's weight cancels out. The model ignores
air, and treats the roll and the braking as happening one after the other,
which is the least the thrusters can do and still land.

Reports call the move a barrel roll, a spin or a kickflip; none call it a
backflip, so the reel shows a roll. Turning end over end in the same time
takes about 4 times the twist (3.9 as a solid box, 4.8 with the weight in
the floor), which is why the caption says so.

The car is a generic two-seater built for this reel, not Tesla's design,
and carries no mark. The ramp, the pad and the camera are made up; Tesla's
own footage is not used.

## How it got here

The creator sent a fan reupload of the demo and asked for a simulation and
an explanation. The first proposal was the fan alone (upside down, it
grips with 1 g); the creator asked for the dramatic part, the fall and the
landing, which is the one idea the reel now carries. After the first cut
the creator asked for the science on screen in real time, g-force and all,
so each panel now carries the car's instruments and the model has both
landings in it. Rendered in Blender
(`scripts/blender/roadster/`); Remotion lays the headline, labels,
narration and sound over the two renders.
