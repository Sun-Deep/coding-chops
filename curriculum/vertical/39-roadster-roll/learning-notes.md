# Learning notes

## Holding on upside down

Upside down, gravity pulls the car off the ramp with 1 g. A fan pulling
air out from under the car pulls it on; Tesla says up to 2 g, which leaves
1 g pressing the tyres into the ramp, the same as a car parked on a road.
That is why it can stop up there, where a loop-the-loop car has to keep
its speed.

## Falling

Let go 5 m up, anything hits the ground in about one second
(t = sqrt(2h/g)). Nothing in a free fall turns the car over, so with no
thrusters it lands on whatever faces down: its roof.

## Rolling over

A pair of jets pushing one side up and the other down twists the car
without lifting it. Started spinning, it keeps spinning, so a second pair
has to fire the other way to stop it the right way up. The jets underneath
point at the sky while the car is upside down, so they cannot brake until
the roll is done; the later the braking starts, the harder it has to be.

## Roll or flip

How hard a body is to spin depends on how far its mass sits from the axis.
A car is about twice as long as it is wide, so spinning it end over end
takes about four times the twist of rolling it along its length.
