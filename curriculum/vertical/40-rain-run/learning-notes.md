# Learning notes

## Two ways a drop finds you

Rain falling straight down can hit you two ways. Your front can meet a
drop in the air ahead of you, or a drop can come down on your head and
shoulders.

## The front is fixed by the distance

Between you and the shelter is a strip of air as wide and tall as you.
Every drop that is in that strip when you get to it, you walk into, and
how many there are depends only on how long the strip is. Run and you
reach each bit sooner, but there is the same rain in it. So running hits
more drops a second, the walkers' argument, and still the same number in
total.

## The top is fixed by the time

Rain lands on your head at a steady rate, so the longer you are out, the
more you catch. At 5 m/s you are out 2.9 s; at 1.4 m/s, 10.2 s. That is
the whole difference.

## When it flips

With the wind blowing the rain along at your walking pace, the rain
moves with a walker, the front count drops to almost nothing, and walking
can win. Into the wind, the front count grows with your time out too, and
running wins by more.

## The check

The counts on screen come from a formula. The script also drops random
rain into the street, 1,000 times, and tests every drop against the moving
body the way a game tests a hit; the averages land within 0.1% of it.
