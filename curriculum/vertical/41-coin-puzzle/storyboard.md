# Storyboard

## First weighing, frames 0 to 149

A complete silver balance stands on a dark stone table. All nine coins are
visible, with the first pair already travelling towards the pans in frame zero.
The headline gives the known-lighter premise and the note gives the weighing
budget. Three coins land in each pan, while three stay on the table. The
balance releases, left pan down and right pan up. The subtitle identifies the
higher side as lighter. Nothing identifies the exceptional coin itself.

The creator approved the full build after the first-weighing preview and asked
for more realistic coins. Tweezers now carry each coin. They lift it above the
pan rim, travel across, then lower it into place.

## Keep three, frames 150 to 239

The three coins from the lower pan return to the left of the table. The
unweighed three stay behind the candidates. The three coins from the higher
pan move forward without changing order. Their rims share the accent to
mean remaining candidates. The arrest lever holds the balance level during unloading and reloading.

## Second weighing, frames 240 to 353

The outside pair from the selected three moves into the pans. The middle coin
stays visibly on the table. The pans balance. Hold the result while the viewer
can still see the unweighed candidate. Keep the camera movement slow; the
subtitle asks which coin is left instead of giving the answer early.

## Answer, frames 354 to 419

The tweezers turn the unweighed coin onto its edge without lifting it off the
table. The camera moves closer to its minted face. The other coins stay
visible and neutral. Only the answer retains orange. The headline names divide
and conquer; the subtitle identifies the unweighed coin. This is a new verdict
picture, rather than a frozen second weighing.

## Look and timing

One dark set, no scene changes, silver metal and neutral light. Orange means
remaining candidates, narrowing to the answer. The tilt direction comes from
the solver; its magnitude is exaggerated for legibility. All coins have the
same thin rim, portrait, lettering and wreath before evidence narrows the set.

Coins and balance fill the content area below the headline and above the
narration. All readable content stays inside the shared vertical safe areas.
The persistent CC mark is supplied by `VerticalShell`.

This shape replaces the default slow-versus-fast race for this cut. A puzzle
needs one continuous deduction and an answer held until the final weighing.
Test it muted at phone size, with no distinguishing cue on the answer coin.

Coin handling lasts for each transfer. Contacts, the balance release and the
answer turn carry their own material sounds. Cue frames come from the same
motion module as Blender. Source peaks were measured before setting gains. No
voiceover, music or generic beeps.
