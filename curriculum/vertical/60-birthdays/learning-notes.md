# Learning notes

## Pairs

Each of 23 people can pair with 22 others, and each pair is counted twice
that way, so 23 x 22 / 2 = 253 pairs. Doubling the class roughly quadruples
the pairs: 46 people make 1,035. That is why the strings pile up so fast.

## The chance nobody shares

Line the students up. The second avoids the first's birthday with chance
364/365, the third avoids both with 363/365, and so on to the 23rd at
343/365. Multiply: 0.4927. So the chance that at least two share is
1 - 0.4927 = 0.5073, a little over half.

## Mine against anyone's

The chance that someone among 22 others shares your birthday is
1 - (364/365)^22, about 5.9%. The surprise in the puzzle comes from hearing
"someone shares mine" when the question is "any two share".

## The rooms

53 of 100 rooms have a match. With a true chance of 50.7%, 100 rooms land
between about 40 and 61 most of the time; a million classes land at 50.8%.

## Real birthdays

Births are not spread evenly over the year. Any unevenness makes a shared
day more likely, so 50.7% is the lowest the chance can be for 23 people.
